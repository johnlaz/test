What do these words mean?
  • Oversold: price has dropped fast or far. Bounces tend to become more likely, but never guaranteed.
  • Overbought: price has climbed fast or far. Pullbacks tend to become more likely, but never guaranteed.
  • Leaning: several checks agree, but the main momentum score has not crossed its line yet.
  • Momentum score (Ultimate Oscillator, "UO"): a 0–100 gauge of how hard price has been pushed down or up recently. Low = beaten down, high = stretched up. It decides the main status.
  • RSI: another 0–100 momentum gauge. Under about 30 = oversold, over about 70 = overbought. Shown in the chart and stock list.
  • Breadth: the share of the index's stocks trading above their 50-day average. Low = few stocks participating, high = most are.
  • 50-day / 200-day average: the average closing price over the last 50 or 200 trading days. The 200-day is the long-term trend line.
  • 52-week high / pullback: the highest close in the past year, and how far price is below it.
  • VIX: the market's fear level, based on how much traders expect stocks to swing. Above about 30 = nervous, below about 15 = calm.
  • New highs / lows: the share of stocks at a 52-week high or low today.
  • Cap-weight vs equal-weight: the normal index counts big companies more. The equal-weight version counts every stock the same. A gap shows whether a few giants are carrying the index.
  • Checks: four tests on each side (oversold and overbought). The more that agree, the stronger the case.
